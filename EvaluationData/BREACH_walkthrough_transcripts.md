## Participant 1

*Background: Tower Defense + Surveillance/Management experience. Cleared Wave 5.*

**[00:01:30] Moderator:** In this video, you focused on the Server Room and ignored the Generator Room for about 40 seconds during Wave 4. Was that a chosen strategy, or was it difficult to keep track of both rooms?

**Participant:** A bit of both, honestly. The Server Room was taking the heaviest path in that wave, so I made a conscious call to keep my camera there and babysit the barrier. But I won't pretend I was tracking the Generator Room the whole time — I'd actually forgotten about it until its warning flashed. The sidebar told me it was still fine, so I let it ride. I'd say it was 70% strategy and 30% tunnel vision.

**[00:02:20] Moderator:** At this point, you turned off the Storage Bay turret and redirected its Power to the Server Room barrier. Can you explain your reasoning?

**Participant:** The wave had thinned out on that side, so the Storage Bay turret was just firing at stragglers. Idling it was free value — I reclaimed its Power and put it into the barrier that was about to get hit hard. To me that's the whole point of Power being allocatable: if a device isn't earning its cost, you move the resource somewhere it matters. I wish the game made that decision faster to execute, though.

**[00:03:05] Moderator:** When the Server Room barrier took a big hit and the alarm appeared, did you notice the UI change straight away? How did it affect your next move?

**Participant:** I noticed the health bar drop, because I was already on that camera. The colour change registered quickly and I immediately started diverting Power toward the barrier. If I hadn't been watching, I'm not sure the change alone would have pulled my eye — it's a bit subtle when you're off-camera.

**[00:03:50] Moderator:** When the Generator Room turret was destroyed on Wave 5, what did you think had happened? Was it clear what you could do next?

**Participant:** I knew it was gone because the icon disappeared, but I honestly wasn't sure whether I could rebuild it or whether it was permanently lost. I assumed permanent and switched to relying on the barrier there. If rebuilding was possible, the game didn't tell me — that's a real gap.

**[00:04:35] Moderator:** Could you tell what was happening in rooms you were not currently watching from the sounds alone, or did you need to switch back to the camera to understand?

**Participant:** Gunfire I could place reasonably well, and the alarm sound told me something was wrong somewhere. But I couldn't tell which room was losing Power just by ear, so I'd have to tab back to check. The audio sets mood well; it just doesn't localise room state clearly enough.

**[00:05:20] Moderator:** Was there a point where the game felt unfair or overwhelming? What was happening at that time?

**Participant:** The triple-prong on Wave 5 came close — three rooms pressed at once while I could only see one. But I'd banked enough Power and upgraded early, so it felt tense rather than unfair. For someone who hadn't prepared, I could see that exact moment feeling cheap.

**Participant:** I thought Power was like ammo — a resource you spend and it's gone. So I dumped it into the strongest thing I could buy, expecting it to be "used up." I only realised later it's allocated, not consumed. That misunderstanding cost me the whole early game.

**Participant:** Not straight away, no. The alarm was too quiet and the icon didn't blink aggressively enough. I noticed it a beat or two late, and by then I was reacting instead of preventing. In a game like FNAF the audio cue is impossible to miss — here it blends into the ambience.

**[00:04:20] Moderator:** When a turret was switched off, what did you think had happened? Was it clear what you could do next?

**Participant:** When a turret went down, my first thought was that I'd broken something by fiddling with Power. I wasn't sure if it was destroyed, switched off, or glitched, and I didn't know how to get it back. That uncertainty made me afraid to touch anything else.

**[00:05:05] Moderator:** Could you tell what was happening in rooms you were not currently watching from the sounds alone, or did you need to switch back to the camera to understand?

**Participant:** I could hear gunfire, and the atmosphere was genuinely tense, but I couldn't tell which room the sound came from. So no — I had to keep switching back, which made the multitasking problem worse.

**[00:05:50] Moderator:** Was there a point where the game felt unfair or overwhelming? What was happening at that time?

**Participant:** Wave 2 felt overwhelming. I was still learning the Power system and the controls, and the game already had enemies on every front. It felt like I was being punished for not understanding something I'd never been taught.
